Goods and Services Tax (GST) has fundamentally transformed how businesses in India manage their tax obligations. Whether you run a small retail shop or a growing service firm, understanding the basics of GST compliance is no longer optional — it's essential for survival.

## Who Needs to Register for GST?

Any business with an annual turnover exceeding **₹40 lakhs** (₹20 lakhs for service providers in some states) must register for GST. Voluntary registration is also allowed and often beneficial if you want to claim input tax credits.

Key triggers for mandatory registration:
- Inter-state supply of goods or services
- E-commerce operators
- Casual taxable persons
- Non-resident taxable persons

## The Three Types of GST

India operates under a **dual GST structure**:

1. **CGST** (Central GST) — collected by the central government
2. **SGST** (State GST) — collected by the state government
3. **IGST** (Integrated GST) — for inter-state transactions, collected by the centre

When you sell within the same state, CGST + SGST apply. When selling across states, IGST applies.

## Common Filing Deadlines

| Return | Frequency | Due Date |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| GSTR-1 | Monthly/Quarterly | 11th of next month |
| GSTR-3B | Monthly | 20th of next month |
| GSTR-9 | Annual | 31st December |

Missing these deadlines attracts a late fee of ₹50/day (₹20/day for nil returns), capped at ₹10,000 per return.

## Top Mistakes Small Businesses Make

**1. Not reconciling GSTR-2A/2B with purchase records**  
Your input tax credit (ITC) claim must match what your suppliers have filed. If they haven't filed, you can't claim your credit — even if you paid GST.

**2. Mixing personal and business expenses**  
ITC is only available on goods/services used for business purposes. Keep your accounts clean.

**3. Missing the reverse charge mechanism (RCM)**  
If you buy from an unregistered supplier above certain thresholds, you must pay GST directly to the government under RCM. Many businesses overlook this.

**4. Incorrect HSN/SAC codes**  
Every product and service must be classified under the correct Harmonised System of Nomenclature (HSN) or Service Accounting Code (SAC). Errors here can lead to mismatched returns and notices.
