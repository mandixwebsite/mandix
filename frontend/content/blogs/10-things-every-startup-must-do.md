Filing your first GST return feels straightforward — until it isn't.

Every year, thousands of startups across India file their first GST return with errors, missing credits, incorrect classifications, or incomplete records. The consequences range from notices and penalties to losing input tax credit (ITC) worth lakhs of rupees — money that was rightfully yours.

> **The businesses that get it right are not the ones with the most experience. They are the ones that prepared properly before filing.**

Here are the 10 things every startup must do before filing their first GST return.





**At a Glance — Your Pre-Filing Checklist**

**01  Verify your GST registration details are accurate**

**02  Classify your goods and services correctly (HSN / SAC)**

**03  Set up a compliant invoicing system from day one**

**04  Understand CGST, SGST and IGST — and when each applies**

**05  Reconcile purchase records for Input Tax Credit**

**06  Know which GST return you are required to file**

**07  Decide whether the Composition Scheme is right for you**

**08  Maintain a GST compliance calendar**

**09  Understand the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)**

**10  Do a full reconciliation before you hit submit**





#### 1. Verify Your GST Registration Details Are Accurate

Before anything else, log into the GST portal and verify every detail on your registration certificate — legal name, trade name, business address, constitution of business, and HSN/SAC codes.

⚠ Common Mistake: Even a minor mismatch between your GST registration and your PAN records can trigger notices and cause ITC rejections down the line.

What to check:

●	Legal name matches exactly with PAN

●	Principal place of business address is correct and complete

●	All additional places of business are registered if applicable

●	Your constitution of business is correctly reflected





#### 2. Classify Your Goods and Services Correctly

Every product you sell and every service you provide must be mapped to the correct HSN code for goods or SAC code for services. Wrong classification means wrong tax rate — which means either overcharging your customers or underpaying the government.

⚠ Common Mistake: Startups in technology and SaaS often misclassify their services, leading to incorrect tax rates being applied for months before the error is caught.

✦ Tip: Get your HSN/SAC codes verified by a GST professional before your first invoice goes out — not after.





#### 3. Set Up a Clean Invoicing System From Day One

Your GST invoices are not just billing documents — they are legal records. Every invoice must carry specific mandatory fields under the GST law, and missing even one can result in your customer being unable to claim ITC on that invoice.

Every GST invoice must include:

●	Your GSTIN

●	Invoice number and date

●	Customer's name and GSTIN (for B2B transactions)

●	HSN/SAC code

●	Taxable value, GST rate, and GST amount (CGST + SGST or IGST)

●	Place of supply

✦ Tip: Use accounting software  that auto-generates GST-compliant invoices.





4. Understand CGST, SGST and IGST

The type of GST you charge depends on whether your transaction is within the same state or across states.

●	CGST + SGST applies when supplier and customer are in the same state (intra-state supply)

●	IGST applies when supplier and customer are in different states (inter-state supply)

⚠ Common Mistake: Charging CGST + SGST on an inter-state transaction — or vice versa — is a compliance error that requires amendment and can complicate ITC claims for your customer.





#### 5. Reconcile Your Purchase Records for Input Tax Credit

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is one of the most valuable benefits of the GST system — it allows you to offset the GST you have paid on your purchases against the GST you collect from customers.

Before filing, ensure:

●	All purchase invoices are accounted for and match records in GSTR-2B

●	Suppliers have filed their own returns — you can only claim ITC if your supplier has filed

●	ITC is not claimed on blocked credits (personal expenses, motor vehicles in most cases)

●	ITC on capital goods is correctly tracked and apportioned





#### 6. Know Which GST Return You Are Required to File

Not all businesses file the same return. Your filing obligation depends on your turnover, registration type, and nature of business.

Return	Who Files It

GSTR-1	All regular taxpayers — outward supplies

GSTR-3B	All regular taxpayers — monthly summary return

GSTR-4	Composition scheme dealers — annually

GSTR-9	Annual return for regular taxpayers

✦ Tip: If your turnover is below ₹5 crores, you may be eligible for quarterly GSTR-1 filing under the QRMP scheme — which reduces your filing frequency significantly.





#### 7. Decide Whether the Composition Scheme Is Right for You

If your aggregate annual turnover is below ₹1.5 crores, you may be eligible for the GST Composition Scheme — a flat, lower tax rate with quarterly filing.

⚠ Common Mistake: Composition dealers cannot issue tax invoices, cannot claim ITC, and cannot make inter-state supplies. Get this decision right before you register — switching later involves a process.





#### 8. Maintain a GST Compliance Calendar

Missing a GST filing deadline is not just an inconvenience — it attracts late fees and interest, and in some cases, your GSTIN can be suspended for persistent non-compliance.

Key deadlines every startup must track:

●	GSTR-1 — 11th of the following month (monthly filers)

●	GSTR-3B — 20th of the following month (monthly filers)

●	GSTR-9 — 31st December following the end of the financial year

●	GST payment — due with GSTR-3B filing





#### 9. Understand the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

Under RCM, the recipient of goods or services — not the supplier — is liable to pay GST directly to the government. Many startups are completely unaware of RCM obligations until they receive a notice.

Common RCM scenarios for startups:

●	Payments to unregistered vendors above prescribed thresholds

●	Import of services from outside India (Google Ads, AWS, Zoom subscriptions)

●	Certain notified categories of goods and services

⚠ Common Mistake: If your startup uses international software tools or cloud services, you are almost certainly liable to pay GST under RCM on those payments.





#### 10. Do a Full Reconciliation Before You Hit Submit

Before filing your first return, reconcile everything:

●	Sales reconciliation — does your GSTR-1 match your books of accounts and bank statements?

●	Purchase reconciliation — does your ITC claim match GSTR-2B?

●	Tax liability reconciliation — does the tax payable in GSTR-3B match your GSTR-1 data?

●	Cash ledger check — do you have sufficient balance in your GST cash ledger?

Filing an incorrect return and then amending it is possible — but it creates a paper trail, triggers scrutiny, and wastes time. Get it right the first time.

A Final Word

Your first GST return sets the tone for your compliance track record. A clean, accurate first filing builds a strong foundation. Errors in the first return, on the other hand, can cascade — creating mismatches, ITC reversals, and notices that follow your business for years.
