Most startups don't fail because of bad products. They fail because they run out of money before finding product-market fit. This guide covers the financial fundamentals that every Indian startup founder must master.

## Understanding Your Burn Rate

Your **monthly burn rate** is how much cash you spend every month. There are two types:

- **Gross burn**: Total monthly expenses
- **Net burn**: Expenses minus revenue

If you spend ₹15 lakhs/month but earn ₹5 lakhs, your net burn is ₹10 lakhs/month.

## Calculating Runway

**Runway = Cash in Bank ÷ Monthly Net Burn**

If you have ₹1 crore in the bank and burn ₹10 lakhs/month, you have **10 months of runway**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Always plan to raise your next round when you have 6+ months of runway. Fundraising takes 3-6 months on average.

## The Three Financial Statements You Must Understand

### 1. Profit & Loss Statement (P&L)
Shows revenue, costs, and whether you're profitable over a period. For startups, expect this to be negative for years — that's okay if you're growing.

### 2. Balance Sheet
A snapshot of what you own (assets) vs. what you owe (liabilities) at a specific date. Investors scrutinise this to understand your financial health.

### 3. Cash Flow Statement
Shows actual cash movements. A company can be "profitable" on paper but still go bankrupt if it can't collect cash from customers in time.

## Tax Obligations for Startups

Indian startups registered under DPIIT's Startup India scheme enjoy several tax benefits:

- **Section 80-IAC**: 3 years of income tax holiday (out of 10 years from incorporation)
- **Section 54GB**: Capital gains exemption when reinvested in eligible startups
- **Angel Tax exemption**: DPIIT-recognised startups are exempt from Section 56(2)(viib)

## Common Financial Mistakes

1. **Not separating founder salary from profit distribution** — pay yourself a market salary from day one
2. **Ignoring accounts receivable** — invoice promptly, follow up aggressively
3. **Confusing revenue with cash** — recognised revenue is not the same as money in your account
4. **No expense approval process** — implement controls before you scale, not after

## Working with Advisors

A good financial advisor for a startup does more than just file returns. They should help you:
- Structure equity and ESOP plans tax-efficiently
- Set up proper accounting systems (Tally, Zoho Books, or QuickBooks)
- Prepare investor-ready financial projections
- Navigate regulatory compliance (MCA, FEMA if you have foreign investors)
